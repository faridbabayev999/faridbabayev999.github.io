Automated Pricing & Market Analysis for Vintage Watch Parts

2026

MSc Analytics Project, RWTH Aachen · Team of 3 · My focus: historical-data scraping and the TMV & turnover models

Problem
Vintage watch spare parts are hard to value: exact comparable sales are rare, marketplace listings are inconsistent, and asking prices rarely match what items actually sell for — so simple average-price approaches break down.
Approach
Built an evidence-aware pricing pipeline that produces explainable Target Market Value (TMV) and selling-horizon estimates. The guiding principle — "retrieval is broad, but valuation evidence is strict": a five-tier eBay search gathers candidate listings, then a deterministic matching and confidence layer decides which evidence is trustworthy enough to drive a price. Historical sold data is preferred with recency weighting; when evidence is too weak, the system deliberately declines to price. Backed by a DuckDB warehouse and a 716-test suite.
Result
Evaluated 728 inventory items → 575 priced (239 on historical sales, 336 on active-market fallback) and 153 intentionally left unpriced where evidence was insufficient; a ~€186k priced portfolio, delivered through a custom HTML/JS dashboard and an Excel evaluation for the client.

Bankruptcy Prediction from Financial Ratios

2026

Machine Learning course project, RWTH Aachen · Team of 4

Problem
Predict whether a company will go bankrupt from 95 financial ratios — on a severely imbalanced dataset where only ~3% of companies actually fail, so raw accuracy is misleading.
Approach
Full ML workflow on the UCI Taiwanese Bankruptcy dataset (6,819 companies): EDA, class-imbalance handling (careful to avoid data leakage), and model selection via stratified K-fold cross-validation with GridSearchCV — comparing Logistic Regression, SVM, KNN, Random Forest, and LDA.
Result
Evaluated with imbalance-appropriate metrics — F1-score, Recall, and ROC-AUC — with the metric choice explicitly motivated by the ~3% base rate rather than defaulting to accuracy.

NVIDIA — Data-Driven Investment Pitch

2026

Financial Analytics course, RWTH Aachen · Solo

Problem
Should NVIDIA (NASDAQ: NVDA) be added to a portfolio? Build the quantitative case — is the return worth the risk, and is the valuation actually justified?
Approach
Computed return, risk, and risk-adjusted metrics from ~545 daily returns (Mar 2024 – May 2026), benchmarking NVDA against SPY, QQQ, and AMD: annualized return & volatility, beta (cross-checked via covariance and regression slope), Sharpe ratio, 1-day 95% Value-at-Risk, and max drawdown — then layered a bull/bear thesis, valuation (forward P/E, PEG) and ESG.
Result
BUY — "sized for the risk." NVDA showed a superior 5-yr risk-adjusted return (Sharpe 1.20 vs the S&P's 0.64) at ~20× forward earnings against +85% revenue growth — but as a high-beta (~2.0), ~48%-volatility position, with China export curbs and hyperscaler capex flagged as the thesis-breakers.

MNL Assortment Optimization — Three Reformulations

2026

Operations Research project, RWTH Aachen · Solo

Problem
Which set of at most c products should a firm offer to maximize expected revenue, when customers across L segments choose by a multinomial-logit rule? This capacitated latent-class MNL assortment problem is NP-hard — the choice probability depends fractionally on the offer set.
Approach
Implemented and benchmarked three published reformulations plus a baseline in GAMSPy + CPLEX (Şen conic, Şen conic + McCormick, Méndez-Díaz, Haase tight). Proved the relaxation-strength ordering and that Haase's switching coefficient is the tightest a valid constraint of that form can have; measured solve time, branch-and-bound nodes, and optimality gap up to |N| = 200.
Result
All formulations are exact and agree on the optimum. CONIC+MC collapses the root gap to ≈0% and solves at the root — ~7 s vs ~110–133 s for the MILPs at |N| = 200. Overall winner: CONIC+MC; Haase tight fastest among the pure linear models.
